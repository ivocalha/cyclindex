    Method

    How it works

    Three steps turn thousands of race results into one number per rider and specialty: each result gets a Level, a rider's best Levels make a Score, and three years of Scores make the Index.

    1. 1

      Every result gets a Level

      Each result (a stage, a final GC, a one-day race, and the points and mountains competitions of stage races) is checked against the 14 specialties. A bunch-sprint stage can count for Sprint, a mountain-top finish for Climb, a breakaway for Breakaway, and so on. Most results count for Overall.

      Where a result counts, it gets a Level from 0 to 100 for that specialty, read from Cyclindex's own tables from the UCI points the result earned. Winning the Tour de France is about 100. For cobbles and gravel, the Level is scaled by how much of the day was really on that terrain.

    2. 2

      The best results make a Score

      Only a rider's best few results per specialty count: five for Overall and Stage Hunt, two for TT, Breakaway and KOM+, three for the rest. The best one counts fully; the others share the same weight again between them, each half the one before.

      Empty places count as less than a real result, so racing and scoring nothing is always better than not racing. The total is measured against a realistic maximum for that specialty. That gives the Score, from 40 (no results) to 100.

    3. 3

      Three years make the Index

      The Index looks at the last three years, counted back from today, not by calendar season. The last 12 months count in full, the 12 months before at half weight, and the year before that at a quarter. That keeps the Index current while still rewarding a rider's record.

      Index = (this year + ½ × last year + ¼ × two years back) ÷ 1.75

    4. +

      Teams and nations

      A team's Index is the average of its 3 best riders' Index, among the riders who raced for it in the last 12 months. A team keeps its history through sponsor and name changes. A nation's Index is the average of its 3 best riders. Missing places count as 40.

    From result to Level

    How one race result turns into a Level, specialty by specialty.

    1. a

      UCI points

      Every result starts from the UCI points it earned. The UCI sets them by race class and place: winning the Tour de France is worth 1300, a WorldTour stage 210, a 1.1 one-day race 125.

    2. b

      Which specialties

      The result is checked against each specialty's rules (the table below). One result can count for several specialties at once, each with its own Level.

    3. c

      Each specialty's table

      Each specialty reads the points from its own table. Sprint, Climb or Stage Hunt reach a high Level with fewer points than Overall, because their best days are worth fewer UCI points.

    The 14 specialties

    Each one is ranked on its own, and together they make a rider's DNA.

    • ALLOVERALLHow good a rider's results are, whatever the race, terrain or role. Best 5 results count.
    • STGStage HuntChasing stage results in multi-day races, on any terrain. Best 5 results count.
    • 1D1.GCOne-day races: Monuments, Classics, Worlds. Best 3 results count.
    • GC2.GCGeneral classification of stage races. Best 3 results count.
    • LOLeadoutDelivering a teammate's bunch-sprint result. Best 3 results count.
    • DOMDomestiqueWorking for a GC leader, credited with the leader's result. Best 3 results count.
    • SPRSprintGenuine bunch-sprint finishes. Best 3 results count.
    • PUNPunchExplosive uphill and short-climb finishes. Best 3 results count.
    • CLBClimbMountain stages and summit finishes. Best 3 results count.
    • TTTTTime trials, and nothing else. Best 2 results count.
    • BRKBreakawaySuccessful, significant breakaway riding. Best 2 results count.
    • MIXMix TerrainCobbles and gravel: Roubaix, Strade Bianche and their sectors. Best 3 results count.
    • PTSPoints+Strength across a stage race's points competition. Best 3 results count.
    • KOMKOM+Strength across a stage race's mountains competition. Best 2 results count.

    Cyclindex rates men's elite road riders on 14 specialties. Every result in the last three years counts: this year in full, the year before at half weight, and the year before that at a quarter. Riders with no results sit at 40.
